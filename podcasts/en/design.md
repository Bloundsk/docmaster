*Written summary of the episode. The audio is produced by NotebookLM from the
learning path itself; this text is therefore not a word-for-word transcript,
it says the same thing in fewer words.*

**First, structure before style.** In a building, the experience is working out
where the emergency exit goes so people get out safely; the interface is
choosing the font on the exit sign. That is why designers start with
deliberately plain grey sketches: fixing a structural mistake then costs five
minutes rather than days of finished graphics. You never compliment a door that
opens the right way — you only notice the ones you push when you should pull.

**Second, make it work for everyone.** Designing for difficult situations
improves everyday use for all of us: dropped kerbs are made for wheelchairs and
help anyone pushing a pram. Starting from a small phone screen forces you to
keep only what matters, which is what keeps larger screens uncluttered.

**Finally, how people perceive it.** Waiting feels much shorter when there is
something to look at, such as an outline of the content being loaded, rather
than a blank screen. And beware of designs built to trap people into clicks
they did not intend: that wins today and costs trust for a long time.

This path is education, not personal advice. Good design is measured by how
effortlessly it guides someone, not by how much it decorates a page.
