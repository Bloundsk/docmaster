it says the same thing in fewer words.*

**First, set your baseline.** Work in 25-minute blocks with no interruption, and
get your tasks out of your head and onto paper. Memory is good for thinking and
bad at storing: keeping every task in mind is like carrying all your shopping in
your arms instead of taking a basket.

**Second, protect your attention.** Switching tasks has a hidden cost. Nine
interruptions of three minutes each can take more than two hours out of your
day, because getting back into the work is what costs. Block time in your
calendar and handle your messages in batches: doing two things at once usually
means paying a mental transition fee every time you switch.

**Finally, move on to lasting systems.** Productivity rests on energy, not just
time, and on systems, not willpower. An hour at your best moment is worth more
than three hours when you are drained. The same arithmetic applies to teams: a
one-hour meeting with eight people uses up a whole working day.

This path is education, and not medical advice if exhaustion persists. Being
organised is not about cramming more into your schedule, but about protecting
your attention for what matters.
