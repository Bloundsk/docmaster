it says the same thing in fewer words.*

**First, protection comes before profit.** An emergency fund of three to six
months of spending works like a shock absorber: when the car breaks down, you
are not forced to sell your investments at a loss to pay the bill. Once that
foundation is in place, start investing early and let compound interest do
its work.

**Second, build your strategy on your timeline, not on your emotions.** When
you will need the money decides how it is invested — not what the market is
doing today. And check your fees relentlessly: one percentage point a year
sounds trivial, but over thirty years it eats a large share of your money.
Low-cost index funds are the simplest way to spread the risk.

**Finally, the biggest risk to your money is you.** We are wired to panic and
sell the moment the market drops. Write your plan down now, while you are calm,
so you do not make costly decisions in the heat of the moment.

This path is education, not personal financial advice. Time, discipline and a
cool head matter far more than trying to time the market.
