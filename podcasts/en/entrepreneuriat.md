it says the same thing in fewer words.*

**First, lay the groundwork.** The most common reason a business fails is
simple: it builds something nobody asked for. The golden rule of market
research is to ask people what they have actually done, not what they say they
will do. Starting without checking is like cooking an expensive feast without
asking whether your guests are hungry.

**Second, prove the business can survive.** Find your first ten customers by
hand, doing things that will not scale yet. And keep profit and cash apart:
profit is the destination, but cash in the bank is the fuel. A profitable
business still stops if it runs dry before its invoices are paid.

**Finally, commit to growth — once the engine works.** Check that each customer
brings in more than it cost to win them. And put agreements between partners in
writing early, while everyone still gets along.

This path is education, not personal legal or financial advice. The real proof
of a business idea is not a beautiful logo or a business plan, but a customer
who actually pays you.
