it says the same thing in fewer words.*

**First, the basic reflexes.** Finding the law is easy; finding the right one is
the hard part. A complaint made by phone is written in the sand, a simple dated
letter is carved in stone. And do not let a shop's fourteen-day return policy
fool you: the two-year legal guarantee goes much further.

**Second, the everyday contracts where your signature matters.** When you rent
a flat, the check-in inventory is what proves that earlier damage was not
yours — and the only real way to protect your deposit. At work, if a contract
offers less than the legal minimum, your signature on it counts for nothing.

**Finally, when things go wrong.** An unfair term in a contract is treated as if
it had never been written. Act before the limitation period runs out. And
before an expensive lawsuit, try free court conciliators, and use your data
protection rights to obtain the documents you need.

This path uses French law as an educational example of how the system fits
together: it is education, not personal advice. In a dispute, victory rarely
goes to whoever is morally right, but to whoever has the paperwork to prove it.
