**First, a language model is not a database of facts.** It is a prediction
engine that guesses the most likely next word — a very powerful autocomplete.
It produces plausible text, which is exactly why its answers have to be checked
before they are trusted.

**Second, a model has no memory of its own.** Every follow-up question resends
the whole conversation, and you pay for it each time. Give it tools — searching
your files, sending or deleting emails — and it becomes an agent. One rule then
holds: an agent never takes an irreversible step without a human's permission.

**Finally, secure and measure what you build.** When a model answers badly,
training it on your own data is rarely the answer: training teaches a style, not
new facts. Retrieval does better — it finds your documents and hands them to the
model to read. And beware of instructions hidden inside a text, which can quietly
take control of an agent.

This path is education, not personal advice. Mastering artificial intelligence is
not about treating it like a human mind, but about understanding its rules:
prediction, memory and security.
