**First, read figures without being taken in.** Learn to tell a figure you can
act on from one that only decorates a dashboard. Take salaries: if nine people
earn an ordinary wage and the director earns a fortune, the average describes
nobody. The median is often more honest.

**Second, analyse without letting the numbers hide reality.** An overall
average flattens the truth; split the data into groups to see where behaviour
actually changes. And beware of mistaking correlation for cause: ice-cream
sales and drownings rise together, not because one causes the other, but
because both follow the summer heat.

**Finally, respect the uncertainty of measurement.** When you test two versions
of a page, checking the results every day until you see what you hoped for is
a trap. And a chain of data is only as reliable as its weakest link.

This path is education, not personal advice. If a figure does not lead to a
decision, it is not a useful measure — it is decoration.
