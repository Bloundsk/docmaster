Learning how to learn — Make Your Study Time Actually Stick

2 minutes · 15 September 2026

Studying longer is not the answer: review early, test yourself instead of rereading, mix your exercises, protect your sleep and build a routine that survives bad days. The Learning how to learn path in one minute thirty-seven.

Open the Learning how to learn path →

What the episode covers

Written summary of the episode. The audio is produced by NotebookLM from the learning path itself; this text is therefore not a word-for-word transcript, it says the same thing in fewer words.

First, review early and test yourself. Forgetting is at its harshest in the first two days. Beware of the illusion of mastery: a text that reads easily is not a text you know. Rather than rereading, test yourself and space out your reviews. Rereading is looking at a recipe; testing yourself is cooking the meal from memory.

Second, mix things up and protect your sleep. Mixing different kinds of exercises, instead of doing a long series of the same one, trains you to choose the right method, not just to apply it. And memory is consolidated at night: sleep is working time for your brain, and an all-nighter is a bad trade.

Finally, build a system that does not depend on motivation. Motivation will fade. Set a fixed time every day, and keep a five-minute version for the impossible days, so the habit survives when everything else goes wrong.

This path is education, not personal or medical advice. Good learning feels a little uncomfortable at first — that effort is exactly what makes it last.

Data & Analytics — Stop Being Fooled by Bad Numbers

2 minutes · 15 September 2026

No advanced maths needed: tell a useful figure from a decorative one, break averages down, beware of false causes and of results you check too early. The Data & Analytics path in one minute forty.

Open the Data & Analytics path →

What the episode covers

Written summary of the episode. The audio is produced by NotebookLM from the learning path itself; this text is therefore not a word-for-word transcript, it says the same thing in fewer words.

First, read figures without being taken in. Learn to tell a figure you can act on from one that only decorates a dashboard. Take salaries: if nine people earn an ordinary wage and the director earns a fortune, the average describes nobody. The median is often more honest.

Second, analyse without letting the numbers hide reality. An overall average flattens the truth; split the data into groups to see where behaviour actually changes. And beware of mistaking correlation for cause: ice-cream sales and drownings rise together, not because one causes the other, but because both follow the summer heat.

Finally, respect the uncertainty of measurement. When you test two versions of a page, checking the results every day until you see what you hoped for is a trap. And a chain of data is only as reliable as its weakest link.

This path is education, not personal advice. If a figure does not lead to a decision, it is not a useful measure — it is decoration.

⏱ Productivity & Organisation — Stop Paying the Mental Transition Fee

2 minutes · 15 September 2026

Productivity is not about working faster: get tasks out of your head, protect your attention from interruptions, and rely on energy and systems rather than willpower. The Productivity & Organisation path in one minute forty.

Open the ⏱ Productivity & Organisation path →

What the episode covers

Written summary of the episode. The audio is produced by NotebookLM from the learning path itself; this text is therefore not a word-for-word transcript, it says the same thing in fewer words.

First, set your baseline. Work in 25-minute blocks with no interruption, and get your tasks out of your head and onto paper. Memory is good for thinking and bad at storing: keeping every task in mind is like carrying all your shopping in your arms instead of taking a basket.

Second, protect your attention. Switching tasks has a hidden cost. Nine interruptions of three minutes each can take more than two hours out of your day, because getting back into the work is what costs. Block time in your calendar and handle your messages in batches: doing two things at once usually means paying a mental transition fee every time you switch.

Finally, move on to lasting systems. Productivity rests on energy, not just time, and on systems, not willpower. An hour at your best moment is worth more than three hours when you are drained. The same arithmetic applies to teams: a one-hour meeting with eight people uses up a whole working day.

This path is education, and not medical advice if exhaustion persists. Being organised is not about cramming more into your schedule, but about protecting your attention for what matters.

Cybersecurity — Replace Digital Vigilance with Permanent Habits

2 minutes · 14 September 2026

No need to be a technical genius: stop reusing passwords, spot the pressure behind a scam, unplug your backups and check the blind spots. The Cybersecurity path in one minute thirty-nine.

Open the Cybersecurity path →

What the episode covers

Written summary of the episode. The audio is produced by NotebookLM from the learning path itself; this text is therefore not a word-for-word transcript, it says the same thing in fewer words.

First, your front doors: passwords. The real danger is not a simple password, it is using the same one everywhere. When one website is breached, automated programs try that email and password on every other site. It is like having a single key for your home, your car and your office locker. A password manager and a second login step close that door.

Second, safety nets against fake messages and ransom attacks. Time pressure in a message is not a detail: it is the main warning sign of a scam, designed to stop you from checking. And a backup drive that stays plugged in is destroyed along with the original; it only protects you once it is unplugged.

Finally, the blind spots. Someone who gets into your mailbox often sets up a silent rule forwarding your messages to themselves: changing the password is not enough if that rule stays. And close the accounts you no longer use — a deleted account cannot leak.

This path gives general guidance, not personal advice. Good security is not about being perfectly vigilant every day, but about a few systems that protect you even on your most distracted days.

Entrepreneurship — Get Your First Ten Paying Customers

2 minutes · 14 September 2026

Forget the startup fantasy: check that people want it, find your first customers by hand, watch your cash, and only speed up once the engine works. The Entrepreneurship path in one minute thirty-five.

Open the Entrepreneurship path →

What the episode covers

Written summary of the episode. The audio is produced by NotebookLM from the learning path itself; this text is therefore not a word-for-word transcript, it says the same thing in fewer words.

First, lay the groundwork. The most common reason a business fails is simple: it builds something nobody asked for. The golden rule of market research is to ask people what they have actually done, not what they say they will do. Starting without checking is like cooking an expensive feast without asking whether your guests are hungry.

Second, prove the business can survive. Find your first ten customers by hand, doing things that will not scale yet. And keep profit and cash apart: profit is the destination, but cash in the bank is the fuel. A profitable business still stops if it runs dry before its invoices are paid.

Finally, commit to growth — once the engine works. Check that each customer brings in more than it cost to win them. And put agreements between partners in writing early, while everyone still gets along.

This path is education, not personal legal or financial advice. The real proof of a business idea is not a beautiful logo or a business plan, but a customer who actually pays you.

Digital Marketing — Own Your Audience and Fix Your Funnel

1 minute · 14 September 2026

Digital marketing is not a money pit but a system you can measure: own your contacts, fix the biggest leak before buying traffic, and keep the customers you have. The Digital Marketing path in one minute twenty-seven.

Open the Digital Marketing path →

What the episode covers

Written summary of the episode. The audio is produced by NotebookLM from the learning path itself; this text is therefore not a word-for-word transcript, it says the same thing in fewer words.

First, know your levers. Search ranking is slow, paid ads are instant but expensive. The lever people underrate is email: a social network is like a rented flat, where the platform sets the rules, while an email list is a home you own, because the contacts are yours.

Second, map the buying journey. Take a simple four-step path: if half of your visitors drop off at each step, only about six percent reach the end. That is not a funnel, it is a leaky bucket. Fix the biggest leak before you pay to pour more traffic into it.

Finally, look at the blind spot: retention. Everyone chases new customers, yet keeping an existing one costs several times less, and a small improvement in how many you keep makes a large difference to the results.

This path is education, not personal or legal advice. Winning at digital marketing is not about spending the most: fix your leaks, own your audience, and look after the customers you already have.

Artificial Intelligence — Prediction, Memory and Security

2 minutes · 13 September 2026

A language model is not a database of facts but a prediction engine: what that changes for trusting it, building agents with it, and keeping it safe. The Artificial Intelligence path in one minute thirty-three.

Open the Artificial Intelligence path →

What the episode covers

Written summary of the episode. The audio is produced by NotebookLM from the learning path itself; this text is therefore not a word-for-word transcript, it says the same thing in fewer words.

First, a language model is not a database of facts. It is a prediction engine that guesses the most likely next word — a very powerful autocomplete. It produces plausible text, which is exactly why its answers have to be checked before they are trusted.

Second, a model has no memory of its own. Every follow-up question resends the whole conversation, and you pay for it each time. Give it tools — searching your files, sending or deleting emails — and it becomes an agent. One rule then holds: an agent never takes an irreversible step without a human's permission.

Finally, secure and measure what you build. When a model answers badly, training it on your own data is rarely the answer: training teaches a style, not new facts. Retrieval does better — it finds your documents and hands them to the model to read. And beware of instructions hidden inside a text, which can quietly take control of an agent.

This path is education, not personal advice. Mastering artificial intelligence is not about treating it like a human mind, but about understanding its rules: prediction, memory and security.

Web Development — Foundations That Last, and Technical Debt

2 minutes · 13 September 2026

The tools change every year, the foundations have not changed in twenty: how a page is built, how it becomes an application, and what makes a site last. The Web Development path in one minute thirty-five.

Open the Web Development path →

What the episode covers

Written summary of the episode. The audio is produced by NotebookLM from the learning path itself; this text is therefore not a word-for-word transcript, it says the same thing in fewer words.

First, a web page is built like a house. One language frames the walls, another paints them, and a scripting language brings the plumbing and the electricity. The golden rule: design for the smallest phone screen first — the lack of space forces you to keep only what matters.

Second, an application runs like a restaurant. It needs a strict contract for how the kitchen and the waiters talk, a filing cabinet to store the data, and a way for several cooks to change the recipes without overwriting each other. And a slow page is rarely a slow server: it is almost always too many round trips.

Finally, what makes a site last is performance, by keeping ready-made copies of files; security, by treating everything that comes from outside as hostile; and tests, which let you change code without fear. Technical debt works like a loan: cutting corners buys time today, and the interest is paid in hours lost to bugs.

This path is education, not personal advice. A good website rests on solid foundations and debts paid back, not on the latest tool.

Finance — Protect Your Wealth from Fees and Panic

2 minutes · 13 September 2026

No maths degree and no inheritance needed: a safety net first, a plan built on your timeline, low fees, and a cool head. The Finance path in one minute forty-four.

Open the Finance path →

What the episode covers

Written summary of the episode. The audio is produced by NotebookLM from the learning path itself; this text is therefore not a word-for-word transcript, it says the same thing in fewer words.

First, protection comes before profit. An emergency fund of three to six months of spending works like a shock absorber: when the car breaks down, you are not forced to sell your investments at a loss to pay the bill. Once that foundation is in place, start investing early and let compound interest do its work.

Second, build your strategy on your timeline, not on your emotions. When you will need the money decides how it is invested — not what the market is doing today. And check your fees relentlessly: one percentage point a year sounds trivial, but over thirty years it eats a large share of your money. Low-cost index funds are the simplest way to spread the risk.

Finally, the biggest risk to your money is you. We are wired to panic and sell the moment the market drops. Write your plan down now, while you are calm, so you do not make costly decisions in the heat of the moment.

This path is education, not personal financial advice. Time, discipline and a cool head matter far more than trying to time the market.

Le bon design n'est pas une question de goût, c'est d'abord une question d'invisibilité. Personne ne complimente une porte qui s'ouvre toute seule ; tout le monde peste contre celle qu'on pousse alors qu'il fallait tirer.

D'abord séparer l'expérience de l'apparence. Un panneau magnifique qui mène à une porte condamnée, c'est l'erreur fatale : belle apparence, expérience ratée. D'où les brouillons volontairement moches, faits à la main : se tromper sur papier coûte cinq minutes, corriger un produit fini prend des semaines.

Ensuite concevoir pour tout le monde. L'accessibilité n'est pas une niche. Les sous-titres ont été pensés pour les personnes sourdes, et servent surtout à qui coupe le son dans le métro. Commencer par un écran de téléphone est une contrainte utile : elle oblige à ne garder que l'essentiel, au lieu d'amputer un grand format à la fin.

Enfin penser au cerveau de la personne en face. Trop d'options fatiguent et paralysent. Et les schémas de manipulation — rendre un désabonnement impossible — piègent une fois, puis détruisent la confiance durablement.

Ce parcours fait de la pédagogie : ce n'est pas un conseil personnalisé sur tes projets.

Open the Droit & démarches path →

What the episode covers

Résumé écrit de l'épisode. L'audio est produit par NotebookLM à partir du parcours lui-même ; ce texte n'en est donc pas la transcription mot à mot, il en dit la même chose en plus court.

D'abord savoir sur quoi s'appuyer. Les quatorze jours de rétractation ne valent qu'en vente à distance ; la garantie légale de conformité, elle, court deux ans. Si l'appareil lâche après un an, c'est au vendeur de prouver qu'il fonctionnait à l'achat, pas à toi. Et un courrier utile n'est pas un long discours : il est daté, chiffré, fondé sur la loi, et prouvable.

Ensuite les contrats du quotidien. Pour un logement, photographier chaque rayure à l'état des lieux d'entrée peut sauver des centaines d'euros de dépôt de garantie à la sortie. Enfin quand ça s'envenime. Une clause illégale est réputée non écrite : même signée, elle ne s'applique pas — signer ne veut pas dire renoncer. Et avant d'imaginer un procès, le conciliateur de justice est gratuit, et rapporte souvent davantage qu'une procédure sans fin.

Ce parcours fait de la pédagogie, ce n'est pas un conseil juridique personnalisé. En cas de litige, la vraie question n'est pas de savoir si tu as raison, mais si tu peux le prouver.

Open the Sobriété numérique path →

What the episode covers

Résumé écrit de l'épisode. L'audio est produit par NotebookLM à partir du parcours lui-même ; ce texte n'en est donc pas la transcription mot à mot, il en dit la même chose en plus court.

Ce parcours bouscule ce qu'on croit savoir sur la pollution numérique, en expliquant pourquoi les petits gestes ne pèsent presque rien, et ce qui compte vraiment.

D'abord la fabrication. Le prix écologique de ton téléphone est déjà payé à la seconde où tu l'allumes : sa fabrication écrase sa consommation électrique. Supprimer ses vieux courriels pour sauver la planète, c'est écoper l'océan à la petite cuillère. Le geste qui compte, c'est de garder son appareil deux ans de plus, ou d'acheter reconditionné.

Ensuite la taille de l'écran. Baisser la qualité d'un film en streaming ne change presque rien ; c'est l'écran qui pèse. Regarder sur un grand téléviseur consomme de l'ordre de trente fois plus que sur un téléphone. Quant aux réseaux, ils consomment surtout parce qu'ils restent allumés en permanence, pas pour transporter ton fichier.

Enfin l'effet rebond. Garder son matériel longtemps est la clé, mais les logiciels poussent à en changer : nos appareils deviennent plus puissants, donc les applications s'alourdissent, ce qui annule les gains et rend les anciens téléphones inutilisables.

Ce parcours fait de la pédagogie : il donne des ordres de grandeur, ce n'est pas un conseil personnalisé.

Open the Négociation & communication path →

What the episode covers

Résumé écrit de l'épisode. L'audio est produit par NotebookLM à partir du parcours lui-même ; ce texte n'en est donc pas la transcription mot à mot, il en dit la même chose en plus court.

Négocier ne repose ni sur le tchatche ni sur l'improvisation, mais sur une méthode qui s'applique avant même de s'asseoir à la table.

D'abord ce qui se joue avant de parler. La question la plus puissante n'est pas « qu'est-ce que je veux ? » mais « que se passe-t-il si je ne l'obtiens pas ? ». C'est la solution de rechange, et c'est elle qui fixe ton pouvoir. Côté communication, même logique : donne ta conclusion dès le début du message, comme on donne l'adresse de destination avant d'expliquer la route.

Ensuite les chiffres. Couper la poire en deux paraît équitable, mais cette poire dépend entièrement du premier chiffre annoncé : celui qui l'énonce oriente tout le calcul qui suit.

Enfin ce qui se passe quand ça tourne mal. Lors d'un désaccord ou d'un reproche, tiens-toi strictement aux faits : ne dis que ce qu'une caméra de surveillance aurait pu filmer, sans prêter d'intention cachée à l'autre.

Ce parcours fait de la pédagogie : il explique des mécanismes, ce n'est pas un conseil personnalisé à appliquer aveuglément à chaque situation.

Santé au travail — Ajuster avant que la douleur s'installe

Le but n'est pas de te rendre plus productif, mais d'apprendre à travailler sans t'abîmer. Santé au travail en une minute trente.

Open the Santé au travail path →

What the episode covers

Résumé écrit de l'épisode. L'audio est produit par NotebookLM à partir du parcours lui-même ; ce texte n'en est donc pas la transcription mot à mot, il en dit la même chose en plus court.

Le but de ce parcours n'est pas de te rendre plus productif, mais de te permettre de travailler sans t'abîmer. La prévention commence par la machine la plus élémentaire : ton propre corps.

D'abord l'installation. Un ordinateur portable posé à plat est une catastrophe ergonomique. Et le footing du samedi n'annule pas deux mille heures passées avachi sur une chaise. Régler son écran à la bonne hauteur, c'est comme régler ses rétroviseurs : deux minutes, une fois, et le dos est sauvé.

Ensuite la charge mentale. Le problème n'est pas le stress ponctuel, c'est l'absence de récupération. Ce qui use, c'est un poste exigeant sans aucune marge de manœuvre. Pense à un élastique : le tendre pour un gros projet est normal, mais s'il reste tendu en permanence, il perd son élasticité.

Enfin les recours. Le médecin du travail est tenu au secret médical et ne travaille pas pour ton employeur ; la visite est un droit. En cas de situation grave, comme du harcèlement, le réflexe vital est d'écrire et de dater les faits au fur et à mesure, parce que la mémoire s'efface vite.

Cet épisode est purement pédagogique et ne remplace pas un avis médical.

How they are made

Each episode takes the ideas of one learning path, in the order that matters. It does not replace the written path: the calculations, simulators and quizzes have no spoken equivalent.

Each episode comes with a written summary. It is not a word-for-word transcript: the audio is produced from the path itself, and the summary says the same thing in fewer words.

The voice is synthetic

Nobody reads these episodes aloud. The voice is synthetic: each episode is produced automatically from the text of the learning path.

That is what makes them possible: fourteen readings would take hours, to be redone every time a guide is corrected. The written texts are written by hand — and they are what carries the content.

What an episode is not

An episode is a lesson, not advice. It explains mechanisms; it knows nothing about your situation or your plans. The paths that touch money, law or health say so in full, and the episode says it too.
