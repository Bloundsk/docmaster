🎨 UX/UI Design

Understanding how to build interfaces that are clear and pleasant to use.

🕒 Last updated: 17 August 2026

The path

Three levels, to be followed in order or picked up wherever you stand. Each reads on its own, but each assumes what came before.

  1. Level 1 Beginner Understanding why an interface works, or does not. No prior knowledge needed.
    • UX and UI: what each really covers
    • The wireframe, and why it stays grey
    • Visual principles: hierarchy, space, contrast
    • User testing, with no budget and no lab

    4 sections · about 10 minutes · no prior knowledge needed

  2. Level 2 Intermediate Designing for everyone, on every screen, without redoing it all on every page.
    • Accessibility, which benefits everyone first
    • Arranging information by task, not by org chart
    • Designing mobile first, and why the order matters
    • The design system: closing questions once

    4 sections · about 12 minutes · assumes level 1

  3. Level 3 Advanced What happens in the user's head, and what to do about it.
    • Perceived performance: real time and felt time
    • Cognitive load: Hick's and Fitts's laws, with their limits
    • Dark patterns, and what the law says about them
    • Measuring design rather than arguing about it

    4 sections · about 13 minutes · assumes levels 1 and 2

⚡ What you will know by the end

  • Why designing for difficult situations improves ordinary use.
  • What makes a menu understandable to someone discovering the site.
  • How a three-second wait can feel shorter than a two-second one.
  • Where the line falls between persuading and trapping — and what the law says.
  • How to put a number on an improvement so you can defend it other than by taste.

Before you start

Warning

This path is educational. The thresholds quoted — contrast, target sizes, response times — come from current recommendations, but those evolve: check the version that applies to your project.

In France, digital accessibility is a legal obligation for public services and for companies above certain thresholds. Most countries have equivalent rules; check the ones that apply where you are.
