⚡ Key points to remember

  • An unfair term is deemed unwritten: it does not apply, even signed.
  • Past the limitation period, the soundest argument can no longer be brought.
  • A formal demand does not interrupt limitation; a claim form does.
  • Many everyday disputes are not economically worth the case they morally deserve.
  • In France the court conciliator is free, and so is the data protection authority.

Introduction

The first two levels served to avoid trouble. This one is about what you do once it is here: re-reading what you signed, checking there is still time, choosing the least costly route, and exercising rights that cost nothing.

It is also the level where we face a question the law never asks: is it worth it?

Warning

This guide is about French law and it is educational. Limitation periods vary with the nature of the claim and have many exceptions: the ones quoted here are the general rules, and they are French. Elsewhere the periods, the courts and the free routes all differ.

Before starting any action, have your deadline checked by a professional. A mistake on that point cannot be undone.

In France, points-justice and law centres give free consultations, and legal aid can cover all or part of the costs depending on your means — the ceilings are revised each year.

When trouble is here

📐 Reading a contract

A contract between a trader and a consumer is not an agreement between equals, and the law takes account of that. Part of what it contains can be set aside — regardless of the signature.

The term deemed unwritten

This is the central mechanism. An unfair term is not voidable on application: it is deemed unwritten, that is, it never existed. The rest of the contract goes on applying.

A term is unfair if it creates a significant imbalance between the parties' rights and obligations. The French consumer code sets out two lists: those irrebuttably presumed unfair, and those presumed unfair unless proved otherwise.

Six terms you still come across

Removing or reducing the compensation owed if the trader is at fault.

Letting the trader change the contract alone, with no reason set out at the start.

Requiring impossible proof from the consumer.

Setting aside the legal guarantee of conformity — common in small sellers' terms and conditions.

Forbidding recourse to a court, or imposing a distant one.

Committing you with no term and no right to cancel.

Three places to read before signing

  1. Term and cancellation. How do you get out, with what notice, and at what cost? That is where the nasty surprises live.
  2. The price, and what it does not include. Set-up fees, activation fees, cancellation fees, annual increases.
  3. Whatever is referred “to the terms and conditions”. They form part of the contract as soon as you were able to read them.
Common mistake — believing a signature is a waiver

“You signed” is the most frequent reply and the least accurate. What the contract takes from the law, the law takes back: the legal guarantee, the right to go to court, protection against unfair terms.

A signature commits you on what is negotiable. It has no effect on what is not.

💡 An unfair term is deemed unwritten. There is nothing to have annulled: it simply does not apply.

⏱️ Limitation periods

Limitation is the period beyond which a claim can no longer be brought. It is not a formality: it is the first point the other side will raise, and it is enough to close the file without looking at the merits.

The general periods, in French law

ClaimPeriodStarting point
General rule5 yearsThe day you knew or should have known the facts
Trader against a consumer2 yearsThe day the sum fell due — an unpaid invoice, for instance
Wages3 yearsThe day the sum was owed
Rent and charges3 yearsThe due date
Hidden defect2 yearsDiscovery of the defect
Personal injury10 yearsStabilisation of the injury
Worked example — facts 36 months old, 5-year period

Total period: 5 years. Already elapsed: 36 months.

You have 2 years left — the claim is still open.

The same file under the 2-year period applying to traders claiming from consumers would be time-barred a year ago. The applicable period changes everything, and it depends on who is claiming from whom.

What interrupts, and what does not

Does not interrupt: a complaint, an email, a formal demand — even by recorded delivery. It is the costliest mistake in the field, because it gives the feeling of having acted.

Does interrupt: issuing court proceedings, an enforcement measure, and the debtor acknowledging the debt. An interruption restarts a whole fresh period from zero.

Separately: a request for mediation or conciliation suspends the period — it resumes where it stopped, which is not the same as a fresh start.

Common mistake — negotiating until the claim is time-barred

Exchanges of letters spread over months give the impression the file is moving. The period, meanwhile, keeps running — and the other side benefits from it whether acting in good faith or not.

If the negotiation drags on and the deadline is approaching, you must either go to court or formally start a mediation.

💡 Limitation is judged on the day the court is seised, not the day you start dealing with it.

🤝 Settling without a judge

Litigation is the most visible route and rarely the most profitable. For everyday disputes several free routes exist — and they are often a compulsory step.

The free routes

  • The court conciliator. In France a volunteer, free, approached by a simple request. They have no coercive power, but the agreement reached can be approved by a judge and become enforceable.
  • The consumer ombudsman. Free for the consumer, and traders must offer one. Their details appear in the terms and conditions.
  • Sector ombudsmen — energy, banking, insurance, telecoms, transport. Effective, little used.

For small claims, an attempt at amicable settlement is in principle compulsory in France before going to court. Skipping that step risks having the claim declared inadmissible.

Worked example — €1,500 claimed, €1,200 of costs, 60% chance

If you win: €300. If you lose: − €1,200.

Average expected outcome: − €300. And that leaves aside the months of waiting and the time spent.

The verdict of the calculation is plain: a settlement, even a partial one, would bring in more. That is not a counsel of resignation — it is what the figure says.

The same dispute before a conciliator, at no cost, changes the equation entirely.

If a judge becomes necessary

For ordinary civil disputes in France it is the tribunal judiciaire that has jurisdiction, with a simplified procedure below a certain amount — with no compulsory lawyer. The employment tribunal handles employment contract disputes, the commercial court those between traders.

Legal aid covers all or part of the costs depending on means. The ceilings are revised each year: check them on the official portal.

Common mistake — confusing winning with being paid

Obtaining a favourable judgment and recovering the money are two distinct stages. If the other side is insolvent or has disappeared, enforcement — which has its own cost — may yield nothing.

Hence a question to ask before starting anything: on the other side, is there anything to pay with?

💡 The court conciliator is free and settles a large share of everyday disputes. It remains largely unknown.

🔐 Your personal data

Since 2018 everybody has rights over the data organisations hold about them. Those rights are exercised free of charge, with no reason to give — and remain very little used. Unlike the rest of this guide, they apply across the whole of Europe.

The rights, and how to exercise them

RightWhat it allows
AccessObtain a copy of all the data held about you
RectificationHave inaccurate or incomplete information corrected
ErasureHave data deleted, outside any legal retention obligation
ObjectionRefuse marketing, with no justification
PortabilityGet your data back in a reusable format
RestrictionFreeze the use of data while a challenge is under way

The request is made by a simple email to the organisation, or to its data protection officer where it has one. It has one month to reply, extendable by two months if the request is complex — in which case it must tell you.

What an access request reveals

A three-line email — “I request access to all personal data concerning me, under Article 15 of the GDPR” — is enough.

The reply is often instructive: the real retention period, who the data goes to, the full history of interactions, and sometimes information you did not know you had provided.

It is also the document that makes a later challenge possible: you can only argue about what you have seen.

When nothing moves

Past the one-month deadline with no reply, or on an unreasoned refusal, a complaint to your data protection authority is free and takes a few minutes online. Silence is itself admissible grounds.

The authority does not obtain compensation for you — it is a supervisory body, not a court — but it intervenes with the organisation, and that is enough in a large share of cases.

Common mistake — believing the right to erasure is absolute

It gives way to a legal retention obligation: a bank, an employer or a tax authority must keep certain data for years, whatever you ask.

Objecting to marketing, by contrast, admits no exception.

💡 One email, no reason to give, one month to reply, and a free complaint if there is silence. Few rights are as simple to exercise.

📚 Going further

  • Your data protection authority — model letters for exercising each right, and online complaints. In France, the CNIL.
  • Unfair terms commissions and consumer regulators — their recommendations, sector by sector, list the terms regularly set aside.
  • Court conciliators — in France, an official directory and free online referral.
  • Your justice ministry's portal — which court, which procedure, what costs, and a legal aid calculator.

One useful habit: before any action, ask two questions in this order — am I still within the deadline, and does the other side have anything to pay with? They decide more often than the merits of the file.
