⚡ Key points to remember

  • A legal answer with no date and no article cited is worth nothing: the law changes.
  • Withdrawal and guarantee are two distinct rights, almost always confused.
  • A period runs from receipt, not from posting.
  • A useful letter is dated, itemised, grounded and provable. The rest is decoration.
  • A dispute is rarely won on the merits, often on the document you can produce.

Introduction

This path does not try to make a lawyer of you. It aims at something more modest and more useful: knowing where to look, how long you have left, and what to write when an ordinary situation goes wrong.

Three reflexes settle most everyday difficulties — a faulty purchase, a deposit that does not come back, a contract you dare not challenge. This level installs them.

Warning — please read

This guide is about French law and it is educational. The periods, remedies and procedures described are French: check your own country's rules before acting. What transfers is the method, not the numbers.

It is not legal advice: it knows neither your situation, nor your contract, nor the facts.

The periods quoted here are statutory ones, stable for years. By contrast, every amount, threshold and ceiling changes regularly: this guide deliberately quotes none and points you to the official source each time.

For anything that commits you — a dismissal, a significant dispute, a notarised deed — consult a professional. Many consultations are free.

The basic reflexes

📚 Where to find the law that applies

2 min The difficulty is no longer finding an answer — there are thousands — but finding the one that applies to you, today, in your country .

The difficulty is no longer finding an answer — there are thousands — but finding the one that applies to you, today, in your country. That is where most mistakes happen.

The two sources that suffice

  • The official government portal — in France, service-public.fr: the official explanation in plain language, with the procedures and the forms. It is the right starting point in almost every case.
  • The official legal database — in France, Légifrance: the text of the law itself. Useful when you need to cite an article, or check that a rule is still in force.

To which are added, depending on the subject: the data protection authority for personal data, the consumer protection body for purchases, the housing agency for tenancies, the social security collection agency for contributions. All publish free, up-to-date sheets.

The three traps of an online search

The country. A good share of French-language results are Belgian, Swiss or Québécois. The rules there are often close — and sometimes exactly the opposite. The same holds in English between the UK, Ireland, the US and Australia.

The date. A 2016 blog post about guarantees describes a right that has changed since. Nothing signals it to the reader.

The source. A forum answer, or an AI answer taken as it stands, can be perfectly written and perfectly wrong. Form is not a sign of reliability.

Telling the compulsory from the customary

Much of what people believe compulsory is not, and the reverse is true too. “You need recorded delivery” is often a prudent custom; “you need a check-in inventory” is a legal obligation.

The test: does the claim cite a text? If so, it can be verified. If not, it is a practice — sometimes an excellent one, but binding on nobody.

Common mistake — believing a signed clause binds

A contract cannot take away a right the law grants. A clause removing the legal guarantee, or forbidding you to go to court, has no effect even signed.

Signing is therefore not giving up. That is the subject of this path's advanced level.

💡 A rule with no date and no article cited is not a rule: it is a well-phrased opinion.

⏳ The deadlines that count

2 min In law, a right not exercised within the period disappears.

In law, a right not exercised within the period disappears. That is not a penalty, it is the mechanism: past the date, the soundest argument in the world can no longer be brought.

Two rights people systematically confuse

The right to withdraw — 14 days in France, distance selling only, with no reason to give. It applies to a purchase online or by phone; it does not apply in a shop. A retailer who takes back an item bought in person is making a commercial gesture, not meeting an obligation.

The legal guarantee of conformity — 2 years, any purchase from a trader, in a shop as well as online. It applies if the goods do not work, do not match their description, or fail prematurely.

The second is far more powerful than the first, and it is the one people forget. It is free, it depends on no paid extension, and the seller cannot refuse it.

Worked example — a new appliance received 20 days ago

Withdrawal: period expired. The 14 days have passed.

Legal guarantee of conformity: still 23 months.

Burden of proof: for 23 more months, it is for the seller to show the goods conformed — not for you to prove they did not.

That last point is the most useful of all, and the least known.

The starting point

A period almost always runs from a precise event: receipt of the goods, receipt of a letter, discovery of a defect. Rarely from posting, never from the moment you start worrying about it.

Hence the value of keeping a record of the date: the delivery note, the proof of receipt, the screenshot of the order.

Common mistake — accepting “the guarantee has run out”

Many sellers reply that the one-year manufacturer's warranty has expired. That is correct and beside the point: the legal guarantee is an obligation of the seller, distinct from the manufacturer's, and in France it lasts two years.

The phrasing that unblocks most situations: “I am not relying on the commercial warranty but on the legal guarantee of conformity, articles L217-3 and following of the French consumer code.”

💡 Withdrawal: 14 days, distance only. Conformity: 2 years, everywhere. Two different things.

✍️ Writing so that it counts

2 min A phone call leaves nothing behind.

A phone call leaves nothing behind. A complaint that counts is written, and it contains five things — eloquence is not one of them.

The five elements

  1. Who, and which file. Your details, the contract or order reference.
  2. The facts, dated. What happened, when. With no adjectives and no reproach: they weaken the letter rather than strengthening it.
  3. The request, itemised. “A refund of €149” gets processed; “a commercial gesture” gets filed.
  4. A deadline. Fifteen days is reasonable and usual. Written down, it starts something running; implied, it does nothing.
  5. The grounds. The article of law if you have it, failing that the clause of the contract.
The formal demand, without mystery

A “formal demand” is not a solemn act reserved for lawyers. It is an ordinary letter containing the five elements above and using the words “formal demand” (in France, mise en demeure).

Its value is twofold: it starts late-payment interest running, and it is the document any conciliator or judge will ask for first — “did you send a formal demand?”

Sent by recorded delivery with proof of receipt, it costs a few euros. It is the best cost-to-effect ratio in this whole guide.

Recorded delivery or email?

Recorded delivery with proof of receipt proves three things: that you wrote, when, and that the recipient received it. Email proves the first and the second, not always the third.

In practice: email for a first complaint, recorded delivery as soon as the tone rises or a period starts running.

Common mistake — writing to relieve yourself

A three-page letter recounting the indignation, the time wasted and the contempt felt reads badly and gets handled badly. The department receiving it is looking for a request and a date; if it does not find them, the file waits.

One page, five elements, no adjectives. Less satisfying to write and far more effective.

💡 Dated, itemised, grounded, provable. A letter with those four qualities does not need to be well written.

🗂️ Keeping your documents

2 min A right you cannot prove is of no use.

A right you cannot prove is of no use. And it is almost always whoever is claiming who must prove — hence the prosaic importance of what you keep.

The retention periods to know

DocumentKeep forWhy
PayslipsIndefinitelyPension calculation, sometimes decades later
Building work invoices10 yearsThe length of the ten-year building guarantee in France
Tax assessments3 yearsThe tax authority's period to reassess
Rent receipts3 years after leavingLimitation of tenancy-related claims
Energy bills5 yearsThe general limitation period
Contracts and guaranteesContract term + 2 yearsLong enough to rely on the guarantee
The document people throw away and regret

Payslips. They are of no use for twenty years, then suddenly serve to correct an incomplete career record.

The check-in inventory comes next. Without it no comparison is possible when moving out, and deductions from the deposit become very hard to challenge.

Digitising, without complicating things

A legible photo of a document carries the same evidential weight as a photocopy: it counts as the beginning of proof, and suffices in the vast majority of exchanges.

A folder dated by year, backed up in two places, settles the question in an hour once and for all. The originals of important deeds — notarised acts, diplomas, civil status documents — are kept on paper.

Common mistake — keeping everything, and finding nothing

A box of ten years of unsorted papers amounts to having no records: the day a document has to be produced within a fortnight, it cannot be found.

An annual sort beats accumulation. And whatever can be found again online — energy bills, bank statements — does not need keeping on paper.

💡 In a dispute the question is not “was I right?” but “can I prove it?”.

📚 Going further

  • Your government's official portal — in France, service-public.fr: procedures, practical sheets and forms, kept up to date.
  • Your official legal database — in France, Légifrance: the text of laws and codes in the version currently in force.
  • Free legal advice services — in France, points-justice; most countries run an equivalent, often through town halls or law centres.
  • Your consumer protection body — practical sheets, and online reporting of unfair practices.

One useful habit: before acting on a rule read online, look for the same rule on your government's official site. Five minutes that avoid most nasty surprises.
