🤝 Negotiation & communication

Making yourself understood, and knowing what can be worked out in figures.

🕒 Last updated: 16 August 2026

The path

Three levels, to be followed in order or picked up wherever you stand. Each reads on its own, but each assumes what came before.

  1. Level 1 Beginner Making yourself understood. No prior knowledge needed.
    • Preparing before you speak
    • Really listening
    • Structuring a message
    • Writing to be read

    4 sections · about 12 minutes · no prior knowledge needed

  2. Level 2 Intermediate What can be worked out before you sit down at the table.
    • Your alternative
    • The zone of possible agreement
    • Anchoring
    • Positions and interests

    4 sections · about 13 minutes · assumes level 1

  3. Level 3 Advanced Difficult conversations, and knowing when to stop.
    • Difficult conversations
    • Biases
    • Recognising manipulation
    • Knowing when to stop

    4 sections · about 14 minutes · assumes levels 1 and 2

⚡ What you will know by the end

  • Why your power comes not from your arguments but from what you get if you walk away.
  • Why "let's split the difference" depends entirely on the first number stated.
  • How a disagreement can cost more than the gap still to be bridged.
  • What the study behind "93% of communication is non-verbal" actually says.
  • How to raise a criticism without the conversation turning into one about intentions.

Before you start

Warning

This path is educational. The techniques described come from published work, but how well they work depends heavily on context, on people and on culture — these are not recipes.

The most widespread claims in the field are set aside when they do not survive scrutiny. That is sometimes counter-intuitive, and it is deliberate.

The tactics described at the advanced level are there to be recognised, not used. And some situations are not negotiations at all: harassment, discrimination and pressure at work call for specific legal remedies, which vary by country.
