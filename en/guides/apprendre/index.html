🎓 Learning how to learn

The one skill that serves all the others.

🕒 Last updated: 17 August 2026

The path

Three levels, to be followed in order or picked up wherever you stand. Each reads on its own, but each assumes what came before.

One piece of advice before you start: this is the path to read first if you intend to follow any others. What it describes applies to every other guide on this site — and to the quizzes that come with them.

  1. Level 1 Beginner What makes things stick. No prior knowledge needed.
    • The forgetting curve
    • Spaced repetition
    • Testing yourself rather than rereading
    • The illusions of mastery

    4 sections · about 11 minutes · no prior knowledge needed

  2. Level 2 Intermediate Organising your work, and letting the night finish it.
    • Interleaving
    • Elaboration
    • Deliberate practice
    • Sleep and consolidation

    4 sections · about 12 minutes · assumes level 1

  3. Level 3 Advanced What transfers, what can be shown, and what lasts.
    • Frequency, or the order in which to learn
    • Transfer
    • Judging a method
    • Building your system

    4 sections · about 13 minutes · assumes levels 1 and 2

⚡ What you will know by the end

  • Why 40 terms learned today come down to 8 within a week — and when to step in.
  • How six well-placed reviews beat twenty-six rereadings, in four times less time.
  • Why a comfortable study session is a suspicious one.
  • How many words it really takes to read in a foreign language.
  • How to sort learning methods with a single question.

Before you start

Warning

This path is educational. The figures quoted come from published work in the psychology of memory and are given as orders of magnitude: they vary with the material learned, with people, and with test conditions.

Persistent learning difficulties may stem from a specific disorder, which no revision method will correct. This guide is no substitute for professional advice.
