⚡ Key points to remember

  • A laptop on a desk necessarily puts either the screen too low or the hands too high.
  • Nine hours sitting a day makes 2,025 hours a year — and Saturday sport does not cancel the position.
  • Looking into the distance for a few seconds relaxes the focusing muscle. It is free and effective.
  • Under French law, twenty minutes of break are owed beyond six hours. That is a floor, not a target.
  • A daily discomfort you get used to ignoring ends up becoming a settled pain.

Introduction

This path is not about performance. The Productivity guide is about organising your work; this one is about a different question: not harming yourself doing it.

This level starts with the most concrete part — the body, the posture, the eyes, the breaks. These are settings with no mystery to them, which hold for months once made, and which almost nobody makes.

Warning — please read

This guide is educational and describes French law where it mentions rules. The legal break minimum given here is French: check the rules of your own country. The physical and ergonomic advice, by contrast, holds everywhere.

It makes no diagnosis and replaces neither your own doctor nor an occupational doctor.

A pain that settles in, visual discomfort that persists, a tiredness that does not yield to rest: those are reasons to see somebody, not things to fix by adjusting a chair.

The body at work

🪑 Setting up your workstation

2 min Most screen-work disorders come from positions held for hours, not from violent effort.

Most screen-work disorders come from positions held for hours, not from violent effort. What causes them is therefore adjustable — and the adjustment holds for a long time.

The four markers

  1. Top of the screen at eye level, or just below. A screen too low tips the head forward for seven hours.
  2. The screen an arm's length away. Too close, the eye focuses constantly; too far, you lean the torso forward.
  3. Forearms horizontal, shoulders relaxed. If the shoulders rise, the work surface is too high.
  4. Feet flat, thighs horizontal. A footrest costs less than a physiotherapy session.
The laptop's contradiction

A laptop has the screen fixed to the keyboard. Placed on a desk, no correct position exists: either the screen is at the right height and the hands are too high, or the hands are well placed and the head tips forward.

A stand and a separate keyboard resolve a contradiction, not a discomfort. That is why they appear in every recommendation, including for prolonged occasional use.

Light

Screen at right angles to the window: facing it, you work against a backlight; backing onto it, the screen throws reflections. In both cases the eye compensates constantly.

A task lamp lights the document, not the screen. And a lit screen in a dark room imposes a contrast the eye tolerates badly over time.

Common mistake — waiting until it hurts

A bad position does not hurt on the first day, nor in the first month. It first shows up as end-of-day discomfort, which you get used to ignoring.

It is precisely because the signal is faint that the workstation must be set up before you need it. Twenty minutes, once.

💡 At a desk it is not effort that harms you, it is the held position. And that can be adjusted.

🚶 Sitting still

2 min Sitting for long periods is now identified as a risk factor in its own right — distinct from lack of physical activity.

Sitting for long periods is now identified as a risk factor in its own right — distinct from lack of physical activity. You can be athletic and sedentary: they are two different things.

Two notions people confuse

Physical activity is measured in minutes of effort. Sedentary behaviour is measured in hours spent sitting. An hour of sport on Saturday improves the first without changing the second at all.

That is why the recommendations separate two goals: move enough and break up sitting regularly.

Worked example — 9 hours sitting, 5 days a week

45 hours a week, that is 2,025 hours over a working year.

The World Health Organization benchmark for an adult is 150 minutes of moderate activity per week. At 60 minutes, 90 are missing.

And even meeting that benchmark, the 2,025 hours of sitting remain. That is the second lever, independent of the first.

What works, in practice

  • Stand up every hour, even for two minutes. It is the interruption that counts, not its length.
  • Take calls standing or walking. With no organisational effort, that moves several hours a week.
  • Put useful objects out of reach — printer, water bottle. The constraint does the work for you.
  • A sit-stand desk if possible: alternating beats either position held all day.
Common mistake — believing sport makes up for it

“I run three times a week, that makes up for it”: it genuinely improves physical activity, and does not reduce the time spent sitting.

Both count, and the second is easier: nobody needs motivation to stand up for two minutes.

💡 You can be athletic and sedentary. They are two distinct measures, and two distinct levers.

👀 Eye strain

2 min Fixing on a close plane for hours tires the muscle that sets the focus, and sharply reduces blinking.

Fixing on a close plane for hours tires the muscle that sets the focus, and sharply reduces blinking. Hence dry eyes, headaches, blurred vision at the end of the day.

The cheapest thing you can do

Look into the distance for a few seconds, regularly. The most widespread formulation is three twenties: every 20 minutes, look about 20 feet (6 metres) away for 20 seconds.

The exact numbers matter little — what counts is the principle: give the muscle a moment of release several times an hour.

Blinking, the forgotten one

Blinking spreads the tear film over the eye. In front of a screen its rate drops markedly, and the film evaporates faster than it is renewed.

It is that mechanism, more than the screen's light, that explains the dry, gritty feeling at the end of the day.

The correction is of the same order: remember it, and take looking-away breaks that restore the reflex naturally.

On “blue light”

The evidence that screens' blue light harms the eye is weak, and the filters sold for the purpose have not demonstrated much on eye strain.

What is better established concerns sleep: light, whatever its colour, delays falling asleep. The useful lever is therefore the hour you stop, more than the filter applied to the screen.

Common mistake — treating a lasting discomfort with a setting

Passing eye strain is fixed by breaks. Discomfort lasting days, recurring headaches or persistently blurred vision call for an examination.

An unsuitable prescription is a frequent cause, and no display setting fixes it.

💡 An eye fixed at close range never rests. Looking into the distance for a few seconds is the simplest thing in this guide.

😌 Breaks that restore

2 min A break is neither a reward nor a waste of time: it is what lets the whole day hold.

A break is neither a reward nor a waste of time: it is what lets the whole day hold. Provided it actually is one.

The minimum, and the rest

French law requires twenty minutes of break beyond six hours of work. That is a floor, and it is often read as a target — whereas it says nothing about how the breaks are spread. If you are outside France, check your own country's minimum: it differs.

And spreading is what counts: several short breaks protect better than a single block. A usual ergonomic marker for screen work is about five minutes per hour worked, spread through the day.

Worked example — an 8-hour day

Legal minimum break: 20 minutes. Breaks actually taken: 15 minutes.

Gap to the legal minimum: − 5 minutes — the floor is not even reached.

Ergonomic benchmark for screen work: about 40 minutes, spread through the day. The gap with common practice is considerable, and it shows up nowhere.

What makes a break a real one

A break that restores changes register. Stand up, move, look elsewhere, talk about something else. Scrolling a feed on your phone uses exactly the same resource as work: sustained attention, close plane, screen.

Lunch taken in front of the screen follows the same logic: it lasts an hour and breaks nothing.

Common mistake — cutting breaks when the load rises

It is the most widespread and most counterproductive reflex: it is precisely when the day is heavy that output collapses without breaks.

The time “saved” is paid for in errors, re-readings and an unproductive end of day — with nothing making it visible.

💡 Twenty minutes in one block satisfies the law. Five minutes every hour protects the person. They are not the same thing.

📚 Going further

  • Your national occupational safety institute — most publish free sheets on screen work, musculoskeletal disorders and workstation layout.
  • WHO physical activity recommendations — the 150 weekly minutes quoted here, and the distinction between activity and sedentary behaviour. These are international.
  • Your national agency for working conditions — resources on organisation and prevention.
  • Your occupational health service — in France the visit is a right, at your request, and confidential. Arrangements differ elsewhere.

One useful habit: set your screen height now, before reading on. It is the one act in this guide that takes two minutes and holds for months.
