🩺 Health & Wellbeing at Work

Not harming yourself at work: the body, the load, and the remedies.

🕒 Last updated: 16 August 2026

Before you start

Warning — please read

This path is educational and describes French law. Minimum breaks, minimum rest periods, occupational medicine, the employer's duties: the rules given here are French ones, and they may not apply where you are. Check the rules of your own country.

It makes no diagnosis and replaces neither your own doctor nor an occupational doctor.

No exercise in this path assesses a state of health. The questionnaires are about working conditions and about procedures — never about symptoms totalled into a score. A mental-health test delivered by a web page would be both wrong and dangerous.

A pain that settles in, a tiredness that does not yield to rest, a mood that no longer lifts: those are reasons to see somebody.

If you are in distress, contact your national crisis line. In France, 3114 answers free of charge, 24 hours a day.

The path

Three levels, to be followed in order or picked up wherever you stand. Each reads on its own, but each assumes what came before.

One useful clarification: the Productivity guide is about organising your work. This one is about a different question — not harming yourself doing it.

  1. Level 1 Beginner The body at work. No prior knowledge needed.
    • Setting up your workstation
    • Sitting still
    • Eye strain
    • Breaks that actually restore

    4 sections · about 11 minutes · no prior knowledge needed

  2. Level 2 Intermediate Mental load, and what makes it sustainable or not.
    • Acute stress, chronic stress
    • Demand and latitude
    • Recovery
    • Disconnecting

    4 sections · about 13 minutes · assumes level 1

  3. Level 3 Advanced The actors, the duties, and what to do when things go wrong.
    • Occupational medicine
    • The employer's duties
    • Burnout
    • Serious situations

    4 sections · about 14 minutes · assumes levels 1 and 2

⚡ What you will know by the end

  • Why a laptop sitting on a desk cannot be well placed.
  • Why you can be athletic and sedentary — and why both count.
  • What separates normal stress from settled stress, and why it is hard to see in yourself.
  • Why it is not a job's demand that wears you down, but demand without room to manoeuvre.
  • That in France a visit to the occupational doctor is a right, confidential, and does not go through the employer.
