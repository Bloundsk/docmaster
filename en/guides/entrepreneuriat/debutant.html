🚀 Entrepreneurship — Beginner

Laying the groundwork before committing anything.

🕒 Last updated: 5 September 2026

📑 Contents

  • Introduction
  • 📋 The business plan
  • 🎯 Market research
  • 🏢 Choosing a legal form

⚡ Key points to remember

  • The business plan shows the project has been thought through rigorously.
  • Market research confronts the idea with reality before you invest.
  • The right legal form depends on the revenue targeted and the number of partners.
  • The financing you choose has consequences for control of the business.
  • Testing an idea on a small scale limits the risk before investing further.

Introduction

The most frequent cause of failure in a young company is neither lack of talent nor lack of work: it is the absence of a market. Something is carefully built that nobody had asked for.

This guide therefore reverses the instinctive order. You do not start with the name, the logo or the legal form — you start by checking that the problem exists and that somebody is ready to pay for it to go away. Everything else comes after, and becomes much simpler.

The legal and tax elements quoted here are French and change regularly: check them with the official sources of your own country before any decision. The reasoning — break-even, cash, testing before building — does not depend on where you are.

The basics to know

📋 The business plan

2 min A business plan has two functions that are often confused: thinking , and convincing .

A business plan has two functions that are often confused: thinking, and convincing. The first matters more. A plan that makes you discover your assumptions do not hold has already justified its existence.

What it contains

  1. The problem — who suffers, from what, and how they cope today.
  2. The solution — your offer, and what it concretely changes.
  3. The market — how many potential customers, and where they are.
  4. The business model — who pays, how much, how often.
  5. The competition — including “doing nothing”, which is your first competitor.
  6. The financial forecast — costs, revenue, break-even, cash.
  7. The team — why you rather than someone else.

The figure every reader looks for

The break-even point: the revenue above which you stop losing money.

Worked example

Monthly fixed costs: €2,500 (premises, insurance, subscriptions, a minimum for yourself).

Margin per job sold: €180.

Break-even = 2,500 / 180 ≈ 14 jobs a month, roughly one every day and a half of work.

That calculation, done in five minutes, turns an intuition into a checkable question: “am I able to sell 14 jobs a month?”. Many projects stop — usefully — at this line.

Cash kills more often than profitability

A company profitable on paper can disappear for lack of cash: customers pay at 30 or 60 days, costs fall due immediately. A month-by-month cash table over twelve months is more useful than a forecast profit and loss account.

Common mistake — the hockey stick

Flat forecasts in the first year then an explosion from the second, with nothing to justify the break. Every financier has seen that graph hundreds of times.

Modest figures backed by explicit assumptions are worth more: “3 customers a month, because I already have 2 signed commitments and 8 meetings booked”.

💡 The business plan serves you first. If it taught you nothing and made you doubt nothing, it was written to please rather than to test.

🎯 Market research

2 min The aim is not to confirm your idea — it is to try to demolish it.

The aim is not to confirm your idea — it is to try to demolish it. If it resists, you have something.

Go and talk to people

Online questionnaires teach little: people answer what they believe to be true, or what seems kind. Twenty interviews of twenty minutes teach more than three hundred form responses.

The golden rule: ask about the past, never about the future.

Two ways of asking the same question

The useless question: “Would you use an app to manage your invoices?”
→ Almost everyone says yes. It costs nothing and is pleasant to say.

The useful question: “How did you handle your invoices last month? How long did it take you? What did you try before? How much do you pay for this today?”
→ Checkable facts, a real budget, and often the discovery that the person copes perfectly well.

A stated intention is worth nothing. Past behaviour and an existing spend are worth a great deal.

Sizing the market

“The wellbeing market is worth 4 billion” means nothing for you. Come down to the market you can genuinely reach: your area, your segment, your production capacity. A small, defensible number inspires more confidence than a huge, unreachable one.

Test before building

  • A one-page presentation with a sign-up button, before the product even exists. The sign-up rate is a real signal.
  • Pre-selling — the ultimate test: somebody takes out their card.
  • The manual version — deliver the service by hand for the first customers, with no automation. You learn an enormous amount, and you have built nothing useless.
Common mistake — asking only the people close to you

Your friends and family want to encourage you. Their opinion is affectionate, not informative. Look instead for people who have no reason to spare your feelings — and particularly those who will tell you no, and explain why.

A reasoned “no” is worth ten “that's brilliant”.

💡 The only test that really counts is a payment. Until somebody has paid, you have encouragement, not a market.

🏢 Choosing a legal form

2 min The legal form determines your tax, your social cover, your liability for debts, and your ability to take on partners.

The legal form determines your tax, your social cover, your liability for debts, and your ability to take on partners. Good news: this choice can be changed. Better to start simply than to delay the project by six months.

The main forms in France

The table below describes French forms. Most countries have an equivalent of each family — a simplified sole-trader regime, a closely held company, a company built for outside investors — but the thresholds and the names differ. Read the columns, not the acronyms.

FormSuited toStrengthsLimits
Micro-entrepriseStarting alone, a service activityImmediate free registration, minimal accounting, charges proportional to revenueRevenue ceiling, costs not deductible
EURL / SARLA family project or one with several peopleA tightly framed structure, protection of personal assetsHeavy formalities, the social status of a majority director
SASU / SASA project meant to grow or to raise moneyGreat freedom in the articles, easy entry of partners, director treated as an employeeHigher social charges on pay

The micro-entreprise as a starting point

For testing an activity it is hard to beat: online registration in a few minutes, no capital, no cost if revenue is nil. You move to a company when you approach the ceilings, when deductible costs become significant, or when a partner arrives.

One warning though: under the micro regime, business expenses are not deductible. An activity requiring significant purchases — equipment, goods — is quickly penalised there.

An instructive comparison

A consulting activity, €40,000 of revenue, €2,000 of annual costs: the micro-entreprise remains advantageous and far simpler.

A craft activity, €40,000 of revenue, €22,000 of materials bought: under the micro regime, charges are calculated on the €40,000 received, taking no account of the €22,000 spent. A company becomes clearly preferable.

The rule: the higher your real costs, the less the micro regime suits.

Common mistake — mixing your accounts

Using your personal account for the business makes the accounting painful, complicates any inspection, and blurs the view of what the business really earns.

A dedicated account — even a simple second current account — from the first euro received. Five minutes to open, hours saved.

💡 Do not stall your project on the choice of legal form. Start simple, change when the activity justifies it: that is expected and common.

💵 Financing

2 min Every source of financing has a price.

Every source of financing has a price. Sometimes interest, sometimes a share of the equity, sometimes your freedom to decide. The choice depends less on the amount than on what you agree to give up.

An overview

SourceWhat it costsSuited to
Your own fundsYour savings, and the riskAny start — it is what reassures every other financier
Honour loanNothing: interest-free, no securityFounders in a support network (in France: Initiative France, Réseau Entreprendre)
Bank loanInterest, often a personal guaranteeIdentifiable equipment needs
GrantsTime spent on the applicationInnovation, regional schemes, priority sectors
CrowdfundingA campaign to runConsumer products, which test the market along the way
Equity roundA share of the capital and of the powerVery high growth projects only

The leverage of honour loans

Often overlooked, they are granted personally, without interest or security, and count as your own funds. Banks take them into account: one honour loan frequently unlocks another, larger one. The French schemes are named above; most countries have an equivalent under another name.

What the bank really looks at

  • Your own contribution — generally around 20 to 30% of the need. If you risk nothing, why would it?
  • The coherence of the forecast — explicit assumptions beat flattering figures.
  • Your experience of the sector.
  • Guarantees — a public guarantee scheme (Bpifrance in France) can back part of the loan, which often changes the decision.
Common mistake — treating a fundraising round as a success

Raising money is not an achievement: it is a commitment. You sell part of your company in exchange for the obligation to grow very fast, and you acquire partners who will have their say — including on whether you stay in charge.

For the great majority of projects, growth financed by customer revenue leaves more freedom and more value with the founder.

💡 The best financing is still a customer who pays. It is never repaid, it dilutes nothing, and it proves the market exists.

📚 Going further

  • Your country's public business portal — business plan templates, calculators, and a directory of support schemes. In France that is Bpifrance Création; every country has an equivalent.
  • Local chambers of commerce and trade — local support, often free or very cheap. Under-used, although it is funded by the contributions you will pay.
  • Honour loan networks — interest-free loans and mentoring by experienced directors. The mentoring is often worth more than the money.
  • The official government portal for businesses — the authoritative source for ceilings, legal forms and procedures. To be consulted before any blog, including this one.
  • “The Mom Test”, Rob Fitzpatrick — a short book entirely devoted to interviewing potential customers without getting flattering answers. The best investment before any market research.

A method tip: before setting up the structure, try to get a first paying customer. That is what validates the project, reassures financiers — and sometimes saves you from creating a company you would have discovered too late had no market.
