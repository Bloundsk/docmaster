🚀 Entrepreneurship

Learning to structure and launch a business project.

🕒 Last updated: 17 August 2026

Before you start

Warning — please read

This path is educational. It is neither legal, nor accounting, nor investment advice. Thresholds, rates and obligations change: check them at their official source.

The legal forms and the employer contribution rates described are French — the micro-entreprise and its thresholds have no exact equivalent elsewhere. What transfers is the reasoning: validating a need, margin, break-even, runway, dilution. The legal figures do not.

For a fundraising round, a hire or an important contract, professional help always costs less than the mistake it avoids.

The path

Three levels, to be followed in order or picked up wherever you stand. Each reads on its own, but each assumes what came before.

  1. Level 1 Beginner Laying the groundwork before committing anything.
    • What a business plan is really for
    • Sizing a market without telling yourself stories
    • Choosing a legal form
    • Where start-up money comes from

    4 sections · about 9 minutes · no prior knowledge needed

  2. Level 2 Intermediate Checking the project stands up before giving it months.
    • Validating a need on facts, not on intentions
    • Setting a price and working out the real margin
    • Going out to find your first ten customers
    • Steering your cash and your runway

    4 sections · about 12 minutes · assumes level 1

  3. Level 3 Advanced The decisions that commit you: growth, other people's money, and people.
    • Unit economics, and why volume does not fix them
    • Raising money, or doing without
    • The real cost of a hire
    • What to write down before things go wrong

    4 sections · about 13 minutes · assumes levels 1 and 2

⚡ What you will know by the end

  • How to check a need exists before building anything.
  • How many customers it really takes to break even.
  • Why a profitable business can run out of cash.
  • What raising money commits you to, beyond the money received.
  • Which documents prevent the conflicts that kill a young company.
