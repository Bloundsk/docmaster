📊 Data & Analytics

Learning to collect, analyse and interpret data.

🕒 Last updated: 17 August 2026

The path

Three levels, to be followed in order or picked up wherever you stand. Each reads on its own, but each assumes what came before.

  1. Level 1 Beginner Reading a figure without being taken in. No statistics background needed.
    • What a metric is, and what is not one
    • Where data comes from, and what that implies
    • Showing without distorting
    • Mean, median, spread: the useful minimum

    4 sections · about 10 minutes · no prior knowledge needed

  2. Level 2 Intermediate Going from the figure displayed to the figure you can decide on.
    • Cleaning: the work nobody talks about
    • Segmenting to find where behaviour changes
    • Correlation and causation: four explanations, not one
    • The dashboard that triggers decisions

    4 sections · about 12 minutes · assumes level 1

  3. Level 3 Advanced Knowing what a figure does not say, and how many observations it would take to find out.
    • Margin of error: what a percentage is really worth
    • Sizing an A/B test before launching it
    • Simpson, survivorship, regression to the mean
    • The data pipeline and its silent failures

    4 sections · about 13 minutes · assumes levels 1 and 2

⚡ What you will know by the end

  • Why an overall average rarely describes anybody.
  • What to check before concluding that one thing causes another.
  • How many visitors it takes to decide between two versions of a page.
  • How a total can say the opposite of each of its parts.
  • Why the weak link in an analysis is rarely the analysis.

Before you start

Warning

This path is educational. The formulas given are the usual approximations, good enough to size a test or judge an order of magnitude — not for a scientific publication.

As soon as an analysis concerns identifiable people, data protection obligations apply — the GDPR in Europe, equivalent rules elsewhere — including to a simple working file.
