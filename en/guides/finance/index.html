💰 Finance

Understanding the basics of investing and managing money.

🕒 Last updated: 17 August 2026

Before you start

Warning — please read

This path is educational: it explains mechanisms, it recommends no investment. It is not personalised investment advice and does not replace a regulated professional. No investment is without risk, and past performance does not predict future performance.

The tax wrappers described are French — the PEA, the assurance-vie, the ordinary securities account — as are the rates applied to them. They have no exact equivalent elsewhere. What transfers is the reasoning: compound interest, fees, asymmetry of losses, home bias. The figures do not.

The rates, ceilings and tax rules quoted change: always check them at their official source before acting.

The path

Three levels, to be followed in order or picked up wherever you stand. Each reads on its own, but each assumes what came before.

  1. Level 1 Beginner Protect yourself first, put your money to work second.
    • The emergency fund: how much, and why before everything else
    • What an investment really is
    • ETFs, and what they change
    • Risk and investment horizon
    • The main tax wrappers

    5 sections · about 11 minutes · no prior knowledge needed

  2. Level 2 Intermediate From principles to decisions: how much, where, at what cost.
    • Building an allocation from your horizon
    • Putting a number on what fees really cost
    • Choosing between the French wrappers
    • Regular contributions and rebalancing

    4 sections · about 10 minutes · assumes level 1

  3. Level 3 Advanced The mechanisms you only see by looking closely.
    • The order of withdrawal, and what it changes to the net received
    • Why a bond falls when rates rise
    • Diversifying for real: correlation and home bias
    • The measurable cost of decisions taken in the heat of the moment

    4 sections · about 11 minutes · assumes levels 1 and 2

⚡ What you will know by the end

  • What order to do things in, and why that order is not negotiable.
  • How to derive an allocation from a deadline rather than from an opinion.
  • What annual fees really cost, with the figures.
  • Which wrapper matches which plan.
  • Why the costliest mistakes are behavioural, not technical.
