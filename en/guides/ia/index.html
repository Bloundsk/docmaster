🤖 Artificial Intelligence

Exploring AI technologies and learning to build intelligent agents.

🕒 Last updated: 16 August 2026

The path

Three levels, to be followed in order or picked up wherever you stand. Each reads on its own, but each assumes what came before.

  1. Level 1 Beginner Understanding what a language model really is, and what it does not do.
    • What a language model is, without the mystery
    • What separates an agent from an assistant
    • Writing a prompt that gets what you want
    • The limits and the risks, legal ones included

    4 sections · about 10 minutes · no prior knowledge needed

  2. Level 2 Intermediate What you need to understand to build something that holds up.
    • Tokens and the context window: the limit and the bill
    • Plugging a model into your own documents
    • Tools: what an agent can do, and break
    • Choosing a model on cost, latency and data

    4 sections · about 12 minutes · assumes level 1

  3. Level 3 Advanced Measuring, securing, and knowing when not to train.
    • Why the same question gives different answers
    • Evaluating a system instead of sensing it
    • Prompt injection, the hole you do not close
    • Train or retrieve: the decision rule

    4 sections · about 13 minutes · assumes levels 1 and 2

⚡ What you will know by the end

  • Why a model produces plausible text, and what that implies.
  • What a conversation really costs, and why the cost climbs with the turns.
  • When to hand the model documents rather than train it.
  • What decides how much an agent can break.
  • How to tell whether a change improves a system, or merely moves it.

Before you start

Warning

This path is educational and deliberately independent of any vendor. The orders of magnitude quoted — context sizes, prices per million tokens — move fast: check them at the source before sizing a project.

As soon as processing involves personal data, data protection obligations apply — the GDPR in Europe, equivalent rules elsewhere — whatever the technology used.
