🔒 Cybersecurity

Understanding the basics of protecting yourself and your data online.

🕒 Last updated: 16 August 2026

The path

Three levels, to be followed in order or picked up wherever you stand. Each reads on its own, but each assumes what came before.

  1. Level 1 Beginner Protecting yourself with the basic habits. No technical background needed.
    • Passwords and authentication
    • Recognising a phishing attempt
    • Firewalls and antivirus: what they actually do
    • What encrypting your data means

    4 sections · about 10 minutes · no prior knowledge needed

  2. Level 2 Intermediate Putting in place what lasts, without thinking about it every day.
    • The password manager, and the single-vault objection
    • Two-factor authentication, from SMS to physical keys
    • The 3-2-1 rule against ransomware
    • What a VPN protects — and what it does not

    4 sections · about 11 minutes · assumes level 1

  3. Level 3 Advanced What is left once the obvious protections are in place.
    • Data breaches and credential stuffing
    • The targeted attack, which shows no crude signs at all
    • The limits of end-to-end encryption
    • Shrinking your attack surface, once and for all

    4 sections · about 12 minutes · assumes levels 1 and 2

⚡ What you will know by the end

  • Why reusing a password weighs more than how complex it is.
  • Which account to protect first, and why it is always the same one.
  • What separates a backup from a copy doomed along with the original.
  • What encryption guarantees, and what it leaves perfectly visible.
  • Why a written procedure protects better than vigilance.

Before you start

Warning

This path is educational and general. It holds for ordinary personal use; a professional, non-profit or exposed setting calls for its own measures and a specialist's advice.

If an incident has actually happened, contact your country's official cybercrime reporting service, which can put you in touch with vetted providers.
